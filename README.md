## How to choose the right plan

Use the following approach instead of choosing only by storage:

1. **Estimate memory first.**
   If you need a database, containers, or multiple services, 1 GB or 2 GB RAM can become restrictive quickly.

2. **Estimate monthly transfer.**
   Separate normal website traffic from backups, downloads, media, software repositories, and inter-service traffic.

3. **Check CPU needs.**
   Static sites need little CPU. Build servers, databases, compression, video processing, and encrypted tunnels need more.

4. **Decide whether San Jose is actually the right region.**
   Test latency and routes from the networks your visitors or customers use.

5. **Confirm the final billing term.**
   The first two San Jose configurations are shown with three-month pricing, while larger plans are listed monthly. The checkout total is more useful than comparing the displayed figures without normalizing the billing period.

6. **Plan administration before ordering.**
   A self-managed VPS is flexible, but it expects you to know how to secure and maintain Linux.

For most general-purpose projects, the shortlist is straightforward:

- **Small personal service:** 20 GB
- **Small application with a database:** 40 GB or 80 GB
- **Multiple services or a busier website:** 160 GB
- **Heavier application or larger database:** 320 GB
- **High-memory or high-traffic workload:** 640 GB or one of the 1 TB plans
